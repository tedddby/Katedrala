Each term adds its normalized value times its weight, rounded so the terms add up to the score. Reach, fan-in and brokerage are log shares, ln(1 + x) / ln(1 + max), against the most a system of 16 members allows: 15 for reach and fan-in, 210 for brokerage. Spread saturates as x / (x + 3). No term depends on the largest value observed, so a score moves with what rests on the member and only slightly as the system grows.

Gravity is a Katedrala heuristic, not an established metric. Three of its four terms are established measures: reach is the visibility fan-in behind the propagation cost of MacCormack, Rusnak and Baldwin (2006), fan-in is afferent coupling (Martin 1994), and brokerage is betweenness centrality (Freeman 1977; computed with Brandes 2001). Cross-module spread is Katedrala's own. The weights are design choices and will be documented with the method in milestone 6.

Entropy is a Katedrala heuristic. Its factors come from established ideas: acyclic dependencies (Martin 2003), propagation cost (MacCormack, Rusnak and Baldwin 2006) and Shannon entropy (Shannon 1948). Their selection, normalization and weights are design choices, and the score has not been validated against maintenance outcomes.

Katedrala will power CodeVerse.

CodeVerse turns a GitHub repository into a 3D city you can fly through: directories become districts, files become buildings, imports become arcs. With Katedrala underneath, the city will show the architecture as well: inferred modules, the components that carry the most load, cycles and broken rules.

CodeVerse is a separate open-source project, and Katedrala does not depend on it. Katedrala already analyzes its source: 3,766 symbols and 14,233 relationships, the same document on every run.
